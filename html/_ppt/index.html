The cleaning strategy is analysis-specific rather than complete-case deletion. Ratings remain ordinal categories, transport extremes are reviewed instead of mechanically dropped, and overlapping daily sessions are merged using interval unions. GeoPandas assigns each valid point to an official ABS Remoteness Area. Population is joined using the state-specific RA code, avoiding accidental joins between identically named remoteness categories in different states.

Association is not causation.

Most of the raw gradient is geographic or compositional; transport distance alone is not a sufficient quality intervention target.

Raw quintiles suggest lower quality at longer distances. After controlling for remoteness, state and broad service type, the association becomes much smaller. The bus odds ratio is 0.965 per distance doubling, while the train estimate is effectively one. This indicates that much of the raw pattern is geographic or compositional. The models remain observational and do not establish a causal transport effect.

Operational capacity shows a clearer gradient than opening hours. Median Centre-Based capacity falls from 66 places in Major Cities to 26 in Very Remote areas. After geographic adjustment, a doubling of bus distance is associated with 1.64 percent lower capacity. Adjusted opening-hours estimates are close to zero. Approved places are not occupancy, staffing or waitlists, and annual hours cover only 60.6 percent of the register.

Interpretation boundaries

The screen is useful only when its limitations stay visible

Time

Denominator

Need

Places are not demand

No enrolment, vacancy, waitlist or staffing data are available.

Access

Distance is a proxy

No travel time, frequency, cost, route quality or car access.

Population

Age fit is broad

Ages 0-13 do not match every offering's eligible population.

Geography

Aggregation hides variation

RA groups can conceal local pockets and small-cell uncertainty.

Decision guardrailUse the flags to commission local validation, not to label communities or allocate resources directly.
The main limitations affect the interpretation, not just the methods. The data are cross-sectional, the denominator is older than the register, and approved places are not vacancies or demand. Distance omits many dimensions of real access, and broad remoteness areas can hide local variation. Small remote cells are particularly uncertain. Therefore the appropriate use is targeted validation, not direct resource allocation.

I recommend three stages. First, validate the twelve flagged groups with local operational and transport evidence, including the specific QA1 standards driving weaker outcomes. Second, improve denominators and add real demand and journey measures. Third, embed this as a repeatable, service-type-aware monitoring process with uncertainty retained. This turns a preliminary analysis into a responsible evidence pipeline.

Conclusion

Use the screen to decide where to investigate next

Geographic inequality is visible across transport proximity, quality composition and approved-place intensity, but simple correlations do not explain why.

In conclusion, transport distance, quality composition and capacity intensity are geographically uneven. Yet adjustment shows that proximity alone does not explain quality. The most defensible output is the twelve-group screen: a transparent shortlist for local validation. ACECQA should use it to focus the next evidence-gathering stage, then design action with current demand, utilisation and journey data.
